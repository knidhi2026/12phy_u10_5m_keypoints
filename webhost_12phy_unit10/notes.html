EVALUATOR’S KEY: 12th Physics Unit 10 (Long Answers)

1. Formation of n-type Extrinsic Semiconductor

  • Doping: A pure semiconductor (Si or Ge) is doped with a pentavalent impurity (Phosphorus, Arsenic, Antimony).
  • Bonding: 4 out of 5 valence electrons of the dopant form covalent bonds with neighbouring Si/Ge atoms. The 5th electron is loosely bound.
  • Donor Energy Level: The energy level of the 5th electron sits just below the conduction band (0.01 eV for Ge, 0.05 eV for Si). The impurity acts as a "donor".
  • Charge Carriers: Electrons are the majority charge carriers, and holes are the minority charge carriers.

2. Depletion Region and Barrier Potential in PN Junction

  • Diffusion: Due to the concentration gradient, free electrons diffuse from the n-side to the p-side, and holes diffuse from the p-side to the n-side.
  • Depletion Region: As charges recombine, they leave behind immobile positive ions on the n-side and negative ions on the p-side. This thin, charge-free region is the depletion layer.
  • Barrier Potential (Vb): The immobile ions establish an electric field opposing further diffusion. This creates a potential difference called barrier potential (~0.7V for Silicon, ~0.3V for Germanium).

3. Half Wave Rectifier

  • Circuit Elements: Transformer, a single p-n junction diode, and a load resistor (RL).
  • Positive Half Cycle: The diode is forward-biased and conducts current. A voltage drop appears across RL.
  • Negative Half Cycle: The diode is reverse-biased and blocks the current. No voltage appears across RL.
  • Output & Efficiency: The output is a pulsating DC. Maximum efficiency (η) is 40.6%. (Note: Diagram plotting Input vs Output voltage is strictly required).

4. Full Wave Rectifier

  • Circuit Elements: Center-tap transformer, two diodes (D1 and D2), and a load resistor (RL).
  • Positive Half Cycle: D1 is forward-biased and conducts; D2 is reverse-biased and blocks.
  • Negative Half Cycle: D2 is forward-biased and conducts; D1 is reverse-biased and blocks.
  • Output & Efficiency: Current flows through RL in the same direction during both cycles. Efficiency (η) is 81.2%. (Note: Circuit and Waveform diagrams are essential).

5. Light Emitting Diode (LED)

  • Principle: Electroluminescence. A forward-biased p-n junction emits visible or invisible light.
  • Working: Forward bias injects electrons into the p-side and holes into the n-side. When these excess minority carriers recombine with majority carriers, energy is released as photons (E = hν).
  • Diagrams Required: Circuit symbol (diode with outward light arrows) and energy band recombination diagram.

6. Photodiode

  • Principle: Converts optical signals into electrical signals. It always operates in reverse bias condition.
  • Working: When a photon of sufficient energy (hν) strikes the depletion region, electron-hole pairs are created. The reverse electric field sweeps them apart, generating a photocurrent.
  • Dark Current: A negligible reverse current flowing in the absence of light, due to thermally generated minority carriers.

7. Solar Cell

  • Principle: Photovoltaic effect. Generates an EMF when solar radiation falls on the p-n junction.
  • Working: Light absorption generates electron-hole pairs. The depletion region's electric field separates them—electrons move to the n-side, holes to the p-side, creating a potential difference.
  • Applications: Calculators, watches, satellites, space applications, and commercial electricity production.

8. Static Characteristics of a Common Emitter (CE) Transistor

  • Circuit: NPN transistor with Base as input, Collector as output, and Emitter grounded.
  • Input Characteristics (IB vs VBE): Plotted at a constant VCE. It resembles a forward-biased diode curve with a distinct knee voltage. Input impedance ri = ΔVBE / ΔIB.
  • Output Characteristics (IC vs VCE): Plotted at a constant IB. Has 3 regions: Saturation (steep initial rise), Active (flat region used for amplification), and Cut-off (IB=0). Output impedance ro = ΔVCE / ΔIC.

9. Transistor as a Switch

  • Principle: Operated in cut-off (OFF) and saturation (ON) regions.
  • LOW Input (0V): Base current IB = 0 → Transistor in Cut-off region → IC = 0 → Output voltage is High (VCC). Transistor acts as an OPEN switch.
  • HIGH Input (+5V): IB is high → Transistor in Saturation region → IC is maximum → Voltage drops across RC, Output voltage is ~0V. Transistor acts as a CLOSED switch.

10. Transistor as an Amplifier

  • Principle: Amplification increases signal strength. Operates in the active region of a CE configuration.
  • Positive Half Cycle: Input VS increases forward bias → IB increases → IC increases → Voltage drop across RC increases → VCE drops (Output goes negative).
  • Negative Half Cycle: Forward bias decreases → IB decreases → IC decreases → Voltage drop across RC decreases → VCE rises (Output goes positive).
  • Key Feature: There is a 180° phase reversal between input and output.

11. Logic Gates Essentials

  • AND: Output is High only when ALL inputs are High. Y = A.B
  • OR: Output is High when ANY input is High. Y = A+B
  • NOT: Inverts the input. Y = A'
  • NAND: Output is Low only when ALL inputs are High. Y = (A.B)'
  • NOR: Output is High only when ALL inputs are Low. Y = (A+B)'
  • EX-OR: Output is High when inputs are different. Y = A ⊕ B = A.B' + A'.B

12. De Morgan’s Theorems

  • First Theorem: The complement of a logical sum equals the product of their complements: (A+B)' = A' . B' (NOR gate = Bubbled AND gate). Include Truth Table proof.
  • Second Theorem: The complement of a logical product equals the sum of their complements: (A.B)' = A' + B' (NAND gate = Bubbled OR gate). Include Truth Table proof.

13. Amplitude Modulation (AM)

  • Definition: The amplitude of the carrier signal is modified in proportion to the instantaneous amplitude of the baseband signal.
  • Key Characteristics: Carrier frequency and phase remain constant.
  • Diagram: Must sketch Baseband signal, High-frequency Carrier signal, and Amplitude Modulated signal (where the envelope reflects the baseband wave).

14. Basic Elements of a Communication System

  • Input Transducer: Converts non-electrical info (sound, picture) into electrical signals (baseband).
  • Transmitter: Amplifies, modulates (superimposes on high-frequency carrier), and feeds the signal to an antenna.
  • Communication Channel: The medium of transmission (wires, optical fibers, or free space).
  • Receiver: Demodulates and amplifies the signal extracted from the channel.
  • Output Transducer: Converts electrical signals back to the original format (e.g., loudspeaker).

15. Propagation of Electromagnetic Waves

  • Ground Wave (2 kHz - 2 MHz): Waves glide over the Earth's surface. Transmitter and receiver antennas must be close to the ground. Used for local AM broadcasting and ship-to-shore communications.
  • Space Wave (> 30 MHz): Travel in a straight line from transmitter to receiver (Line-of-Sight propagation). Used in Television, satellite communication, and RADAR.

16. Frequency Modulation (FM): Advantages and Limitations

  • Advantages: Large decrease in noise (better signal-to-noise ratio), high transmission efficiency, and better audio quality due to wide operating bandwidth.
  • Limitations: Requires a much wider channel, FM transmitters/receivers are more complex and costly, and it covers less geographical area compared to AM.

17. Satellite Communication

  • Definition: The mode of signal transmission between a transmitter and a receiver via an orbiting satellite. Uses an uplink (to satellite, ~6 GHz) and a downlink (from satellite, ~4 GHz) through a transponder.
  • Applications:
    • Weather monitoring: Predicting rain, hurricanes, cyclones.
    • Communication: TV, radio, and internet signals.
    • Navigation: Geographic location positioning (GPS).
