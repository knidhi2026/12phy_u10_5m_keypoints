🧲 Unit 3: Magnetism & Magnetic Effects of Current

Long Answer Questions (5 marks) – Complete key notes

1. Discuss Earth's magnetic field in detail.

  • Introduction: Earth behaves as a giant bar magnet. The north pole of a compass needle points toward geographic north, which is actually the magnetic south pole.
  • Geomagnetism: Branch of physics dealing with Earth's magnetic field.

Three magnetic elements (required to specify the field):

  1. Magnetic declination (D): Angle between magnetic meridian and geographic meridian. (Chennai: −1°16′)
  2. Magnetic dip or inclination (I): Angle made by total field B with horizontal. (Chennai: 14°28′)
  3. Horizontal component (BH): Component along horizontal in magnetic meridian.
BH = BE cos I    BV = BE sin I    tan I = BV / BH

Special cases:

  • At magnetic equator: I = 0°, BH = BE, BV = 0
  • At magnetic poles: I = 90°, BH = 0, BV = BE

2. Magnetic field due to infinitely long straight conductor (Biot‑Savart law).

Consider an infinitely long wire YY' with current I. Point P at perpendicular distance a.
Take a small element dl (AB). Biot‑Savart law: dB = (µ₀/4π)·(I dl sinθ)/r².
From geometry: dl sinθ = r dφ, and cosφ = a/r ⇒ r = a / cosφ.
Substitute: dB = (µ₀/4π)·(I / a) cosφ dφ. Direction into page (⊗).
Integrate φ from −π/2 to π/2: B = (µ₀ I / 4π a) [sinφ] from −π/2 to π/2 = (µ₀ I / 4π a)·(1 − (−1)).
B = µ₀ I / (2π a)    (or B = µ₀ I / (2π r))

Direction: concentric circles around wire (right‑hand thumb rule).

3. Magnetic field on the axis of a circular coil (Biot‑Savart).

Circular coil radius R, current I. Point P on axis at distance z from centre.
Consider element dl at C. dB = (µ₀/4π)·(I dl)/r² (θ = 90°).
Resolve: horizontal components cancel, vertical components add. dBz = dB sinφ.
sinφ = R / √(R²+z²) , r² = R²+z².
Integrate over whole loop: B = ∫ (µ₀ I / 4π)·(R dl) / (R²+z²)3/2.
∫ dl = 2πR ⇒ B = (µ₀ I R²) / [2(R²+z²)3/2].
For N turns: B = (µ₀ N I R²) / [2(R²+z²)3/2]

At centre (z=0): B = µ₀ N I / (2R).

4. Torque on a magnetic needle (bar magnet) in uniform field.

Bar magnet length 2l, pole strength qm, magnetic moment pm = qm·2l.
Forces: on N‑pole FN = qmB, on S‑pole FS = –qmB. Net force = 0.
These forces form a couple. Torque about centre: τ = force × perpendicular distance.
τ = (l sinθ)·(qmB) + (l sinθ)·(qmB) = 2l qmB sinθ = pm B sinθ.
τ = pm B sinθ   (vector: τ = pm × B)

Maximum at θ = 90°, zero at θ = 0° or 180°. Torque aligns magnet with field.

5. Magnetic field on axial line of a bar magnet.

Magnet NS, length 2l, pole strength qm. Point C on axis at distance r from centre.
Field due to N‑pole at C: BN = (µ₀/4π)·qm/(r−l)² (away from N).
Field due to S‑pole at C: BS = – (µ₀/4π)·qm/(r+l)² (toward S).
Net B = BN + BS = (µ₀ qm/4π)[1/(r−l)² – 1/(r+l)²].
Simplify using (r+l)² − (r−l)² = 4rl ⇒ B = (µ₀/4π)·[4r l qm / (r²−l²)²].
pm = qm·2l ⇒ B = (µ₀/4π)·[2r pm / (r²−l²)²].
For r ≫ l: (r²−l²)² ≈ r⁴ ⇒ Baxial = (µ₀/4π)·(2pm/r³).
Baxial = (µ₀/4π)·(2pm/r³)   (direction along pm)

6. Magnetic field on equatorial line of a bar magnet.

Same magnet. Point C on equatorial line at distance r from centre.
Distance to each pole: r' = √(r²+l²). Field magnitudes equal: BN = BS = (µ₀/4π)·qm/r'².
Resolve: horizontal components cancel, vertical components add opposite → net B = –2BNcosθ î.
cosθ = l/r' ⇒ B = – (µ₀/4π)·(2qm l)/(r²+l²)3/2.
pm = 2qml ⇒ B = – (µ₀/4π)·pm/(r²+l²)3/2.
For r ≫ l: Bequatorial = – (µ₀/4π)·(pm/r³).
Beq = (µ₀/4π)·(pm/r³)   (magnitude), direction opposite pm.

Relation: Baxial = 2 Bequatorial.

7. Magnetic field due to long straight conductor using Ampere's circuital law.

Consider infinite straight wire with current I. By symmetry, B is tangential, constant on circle radius r.
Choose circular Amperian loop of radius r. Ampere's law: ∮ B·dl = µ₀ Ienclosed.
B parallel dl ⇒ ∮ B dl = B·(2πr) = µ₀ I.
B = µ₀ I / (2πr)

Same as Biot‑Savart result. Vector: B = (µ₀ I / 2πr) n (tangent direction).

8. Working of cyclotron – principle, construction, working, limitations.

Principle: Charged particle moving ⊥ to magnetic field experiences Lorentz force that provides centripetal force → circular motion.

Construction:

  • Two hollow D‑shaped electrodes (Dees) in vacuum.
  • Uniform magnetic field ⊥ to plane of Dees.
  • High‑frequency oscillator connected to Dees.
  • Source at centre, deflector plate at edge.

Working:

  1. Ion from source accelerated towards Dee (negative at that instant).
  2. Inside Dee, only magnetic field acts: radius r = mv/qB.
  3. After half‑circle, polarity reverses → ion accelerated across gap.
  4. Velocity increases → radius increases → spiral path.
  5. Finally beam extracted by deflector.
Cyclotron frequency f = qB/(2πm)    Time period T = 2πm/(qB)
Kinetic energy = q²B²r²/(2m)

Resonance condition: oscillator frequency equals cyclotron frequency.

Limitations: relativistic mass increase at high speed; electrons not accelerated (very high frequency); neutral particles cannot be accelerated.

9. Tangent law and Tangent Galvanometer.

Tangent law: When a magnet is freely suspended in two mutually perpendicular uniform magnetic fields, it rests along the resultant: B = BH tan θ.

Tangent Galvanometer (TG): moving‑magnet type, works on tangent law.

Construction: circular coil (N turns, radius R), compass box with magnetic needle, aluminium pointer, mirror to avoid parallax.

Theory: B due to coil = µ₀ N I / (2R). At equilibrium: µ₀ N I/(2R) = BH tan θ ⇒ BH = (µ₀ N / 2R)·(I / tan θ).

Precautions: no magnetic materials nearby, coil vertical in magnetic meridian, pointer reads 0°‑0° with no current.

Sensitivity: most sensitive at θ = 45°, readings taken between 30° and 60°.

10. Torque on a current‑carrying coil in a magnetic field.

Rectangular coil PQRS (length a, breadth b), N turns, current I, placed in uniform B.
Forces on PQ and RS: F = I a B, opposite directions, form a couple.
Torque on PQ about axis = (b/2 sinθ)·I a B ; same for RS.
Total τ = 2 × (b/2 sinθ) I a B = I a b B sinθ = I A B sinθ (A = ab).
For N turns: τ = N I A B sinθ.
τ = N I A B sinθ   (vector: τ = (N I A) × B)

Using magnetic moment pm = N I A ⇒ τ = pm B sinθ.

Maximum at θ = 90°, zero at θ = 0° or 180°.

11. Conversion of galvanometer into ammeter and voltmeter.

Into Ammeter: low resistance (shunt S) in parallel.

S = Ig Rg / (I − Ig)    Ra = RgS/(Rg+S)

For n‑fold range (I = n Ig): S = Rg/(n−1).

Into Voltmeter: high resistance Rh in series.

Rh = V/Ig − Rg    Rv = Rg + Rh

For n‑fold range (V = n IgRg): Rh = (n−1)Rg.

12. Magnetic field inside/outside a long solenoid (Ampere's law).

Solenoid length L, N turns, current I. Turns per unit length n = N/L.
Consider rectangular Amperian loop abcd of length h. Along ab (inside): B parallel; bc and da: B ⟂ dl ⇒ zero; cd (outside): B ≈ 0.
∮ B·dl = B·h = µ₀ × (current enclosed). Current enclosed = n·h·I.
Thus B·h = µ₀ n h I ⇒ B = µ₀ n I.
Binside = µ₀ n I    (uniform, axial direction)

Outside solenoid: B ≈ 0. With iron core: B = µ₀ µr n I.

13. Force between two parallel current‑carrying conductors.

Two long parallel wires A and B, separation r, currents I₁, I₂ (same direction).
Field at B due to A: B₁ = µ₀ I₁/(2πr) (into page ⊗).
Force on length l of B: F = I₂ l B₁ sin90° = I₂ l (µ₀ I₁/(2πr)).
Direction: using left‑hand rule, force on B is toward A (attractive).
Similarly, force on A toward B. Force per unit length: F/l = µ₀ I₁ I₂/(2πr).
F/l = µ₀ I₁ I₂ / (2πr)

Same direction → attract; opposite direction → repel.

Definition of ampere: 1 A is that constant current which, when flowing in two infinitely long parallel conductors 1 m apart in vacuum, produces a force of 2×10⁻⁷ N per metre.

14. Magnetic Lorentz force – account and motion in field.

Lorentz force: F = q(E + v×B). Magnetic part: Fm = q(v×B).

Fm = q v B sinθ
  • Direction ⊥ to both v and B (right‑hand rule).
  • Work done by magnetic force = 0 (power = 0).

Motion:

  • v ⟂ B: circular path. Radius r = mv/(qB); period T = 2πm/(qB) (independent of speed).
  • v at angle θ: helical path with pitch p = v∥·T = (v cosθ)·(2πm/qB).

Definition of tesla: 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹.

16. Force on a current‑carrying conductor in magnetic field – derivation.

Conductor length dl, area A, free electron density n, drift velocity vd. Current I = n e A vd.
Force on one electron: f = –e (vd×B).
Number of electrons in element = n A dl. Total force dF = (n A dl)·(–e vd×B).
But –n e A vd dl = I dl (since current direction opposite electron flow). Hence dF = I dl × B.
For straight conductor length l: F = I l B sinθ.
F = I l × B   (magnitude F = B I l sinθ)

Fleming's left‑hand rule: thumb – force, forefinger – field, middle finger – current.

17. Moving coil galvanometer – principle, construction, working.

Principle: A current‑carrying coil in a uniform magnetic field experiences torque.

Construction: Rectangular coil (N turns) wound on soft iron core, suspended between poles of permanent magnet (radial field). Phosphor‑bronze suspension and hair spring provide restoring torque. Mirror for lamp‑scale reading.

Working: Current I causes deflecting torque τd = N A B I. Suspension wire provides restoring torque τr = K θ. At equilibrium, N A B I = K θ ⇒ I = (K / N A B) θ = G θ (G = galvanometer constant).

I ∝ θ ⇒ linear scale.

Current sensitivity: Is = θ/I = N A B / K.

Voltage sensitivity: Vs = θ/V = N A B / (K Rg).

Increasing sensitivity: increase N, B, A; decrease K (use phosphor‑bronze).
